# Hearthwild settlement economy and art plan

November 10, 2026. The [master plan](INCENTIVE_FIRST_MASTER_PLAN.md), [request audit](REQUEST_AUDIT.md), and [QA backlog](QA_BACKLOG_2026-11-03.md) retain the full campaign scope. These phases develop their farming, settlement, civic, conflict and art requirements.

## 1. Farms and food supply

**Current game.** An 8.2 × 8.2 m field represents 67.24 m². With neutral site and water multipliers, one fully tended field has a nominal potential of 260 clean-grain-equivalent units. The formula scales by area, planting, tending, crop, averaged soil/habitat, fertility, drought and the applicable age bonus. Neutral hand labor is 45 simulation worker-seconds sowing, 55 tending and about 26 reaping a 260-unit crop, before travel, carrying and processing. Better soil principally raises output. It raises reaping and downstream labor as more crop has to be handled, while sowing and tending still scale with area.

A marked cereal field produces two sheaves per potential grain unit. Eight sheaves become six rough grain plus straw, then four clean-grain equivalents less an 8% seed reserve. Clean grain needs processing for ordinary meals. Emergency raw grain feeds at one-tenth value. With no travel or losses, the nominal field produces about 239 clean-grain units after seed reservation. A standard adult at base consumption needs 64.8 food units per 1,800 simulation seconds. Thus the nominal farm is sized like several adult-years of potential grain before losses, despite its small rendered footprint. That is a game scale. At present, small batches are the limiting factor. Fully processing that harvest can call for 65 thresh/winnow cycles and 59 grind/bake cycles, totaling about 4,130 hands-on worker-seconds at the dedicated stations before hauling, rain or worker skill. Increasing planted area alone can make a backlog worse.

**Historical calibration.** In the Winchester accounts, 79.6% of the recorded wheat yields in 1209–1349 were below 12 bushels per acre. The accounts support separating gross yield, seed return and net output. An illustrative 8–12 bushels/acre at the historical study's 22.39 kg/bushel is about 0.44–0.66 t/ha, or about 3–4.5 kg on 67.24 m². Those are comparative figures for that region and measure. A separate early nineteenth-century comparison gives reaping alone about 7.2 person-days/ha in England and 9.3–16.3 in France. Those figures exclude tillage, threshing and milling. The FAO's hand-tool comparison reports large differences between skilled scythe use and sickle reaping. These sources anchor differences between tasks and methods across particular contexts. [Winchester yield tables](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805210/83492/sample/9780521083492ws.pdf), [historical unit conversion](https://www.bahs.org.uk/AGHR/VOLUMES/AGHR38.pdf), [Allen's labor comparison](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/rest/bitstreams/12243/retrieve), [FAO hand-harvesting practices](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1619243/)

**Implementation.**

1. Expose cropped m², soil quality, fertility, yield per m², work per operation and the current limiting station. Record active work, travel, spoiled stock, reserved seed and delivered edible output separately. Use units of food and worker-seconds in the UI. Keep a research conversion table outside the simulation.
2. Compare the current chain to a variant where a larger threshing yard, mill house and bakery handle four small batches with roughly 1.5–2× the active seconds per staffed station cycle as an initial tuning range. Real stock must arrive in physically sized loads. Each station queues and reserves them once. Teach the scale-up after successful smaller batches, with enough space, materials, worker positions and fuel. Prototype a simple cooked-grain meal so early households can get value from clean grain before they have a bakery.
3. Model soil depth, moisture, flood risk and fertility separately. Give river terraces an advantage in moderate moisture and a downside on exposed floodplains. Let drainage, fallow, pulses and finite manure alter the relevant factor. Add a modest soil-workability modifier to cultivation labor and a slope/terrain cost to travel and soil preparation. Start with a workability range of 0.8–1.3×, then tune it by observed behavior. Tools and traction change the operations they actually help.
4. Benchmark identical plots and populations at 10, 30 and 100 residents over multiple seasons. Compare successful meal output per worker-minute, labor share, losses, time to clear an autumn harvest and food gaps. Include small mixed villages with hand tools and a city that buys food. Keep the current yield scale until the physical chain and seasonal labor balance pass. Then tune yield, effort and haulage together on the same saves.

## 2. Local economies and expansion

A culture can contain several settlements. Use a saved settlement ID separate from culture/team and link residences, buildings, stores and work orders to it. Assign site-owned stocks, physical transfers and resident home locations. Provide an aggregate culture summary by summing settlement ledgers. Keep existing saves as a single primary settlement until an actual second site is founded. Grow this out of existing outposts, founding journeys, recurring trade, influence, and paid building projects.

Start with mixed settlements. A village gains a specialization as it repeatedly delivers a distinctive surplus, employs trained workers and develops the needed equipment. The player can prioritize a role and fund its facilities. Record work seconds, delivery quantity, quality, throughput, reliability and local reserve. A specialty earns efficiencies from shorter routes, apprentices, fixtures and consolidated loads. Repeated deliveries build reputation with buyers that witnessed them. Local food and safety reserves take priority during shortages. Household and regional production for exchange can exist before highly centralized cities. [Archaeological evidence of specialization and exchange in Oaxaca](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/109/20/7617.full.pdf)

| Settlement | Evidence and attraction | Local dependence |
|---|---|---|
| Grain district | Deep soil, seasonal freshwater, milling throughput and exported meals | Tools, timber, secure winter carriage |
| Fishery and port | Recovering fish grounds, dry-bank access, docks and repeat shipments | Fuel, rope, dry storage, safe water routes |
| Woodland settlement | Reachable timber, sustainable regrowth, charcoal and wagon loading | Imported food when local soil is poor |
| Quarry or metal town | Finite exposed deposits, maintained haul road, workshop practice | Water, fuel, meals and outside buyers |
| Craft or fair town | Clay, textiles, other real artisan output and witnessed merchant traffic | Inputs and buyers at accessible neighboring sites |
| Fort or crossing | Holds a traversable pass, bridge or landing that useful routes actually cross | Garrison food, repairs and political trust |

Show expansion opportunities as surveyed reasons and expected travel time. Rate a candidate by independent water, shelter, attainable food, usable land, resource advantage, useful network position and safety. Deduct startup cargo, travel, congestion and exposure. Use physical households and supplies to found a satellite. Land routes require reachable paths, and sea routes require hull, berth, provisions and crew. Pioneer groups can turn back when reserves run low.

Use soft capacity based on the limiting resource. Review sustained crowding through household space, safe water per person, seasonal meal coverage, walking time and durable jobs. As a test band, flag a site when projected demand exceeds 85% of one local capacity for two reviews, and start voluntary relocation offers when there is sustained pressure and a known better site. A market town can sustain hundreds with water, housing, imports and storage. Small settlements can stay viable through trade. Overcrowding may also be solved by better service, sanitation or housing. Report the real constraint alongside a move proposal and allow rejection.

Test a single self-sufficient village, two complementary villages, a 150-person city with satellites, then a 300-person city with at least three supplier towns. Save with active transfers, reload, sever one route, and verify loss or rerouting and no duplicated cargo. Measure food gaps, average commute, skill retention, trade reliability, migration flows and the marginal benefit of a new settlement.

## 3. Prestige, civic works and cultural identity

Current prestige derives from residents, claimed regions, age, buildings and alliances. Civic prestige should reward completed, used and maintained landmarks, with separate local attachment and external renown. Start with the existing cairn, grove, temple and university. A landmark has an auditable value, condition, participation count and known witnesses. Stack identical types with diminishing returns. Add earned stories and standing from relief after disasters, craftsmanship, public gatherings and completed infrastructure. Keep territorial influence separate. Allow external renown to improve recruitment, introductions or bargaining through known networks, with modest caps and no instant stock or territorial reward.

A large work proceeds through survey, clearing and footing, materials staging, construction, finishing and opening. Each phase has finite material, real transport, slots and paid worker time. Use cranes, ramps, sledges or boats only after demonstrated techniques and matching terrain. Show actual scaffolds, exposed structure and completed sections. Let players cap labor or reserve critical stocks while building. Landmark art needs a distinct outline at mid zoom, identifiable construction joints at close zoom and a small pool of repeatable modules. Spend geometry on silhouettes, entrances and recognizable public spaces. Dawn of Man's hauling mechanics illustrate how a monument can create a labor and transport decision. The archaeology provides principles for original designs. [Dawn of Man megalith logistics](https://dawnofman.fandom.com/wiki/Megalith), [The Met on Karnak construction](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/essays/art-architecture-and-the-city-in-the-reign-of-amenhotep-iv-akhenaten-ca-13531336-b-c)

| Reference family | Original Hearthwild project | Eligibility and function |
|---|---|---|
| Egyptian Nile valley | Flood Stair and Processional Court | Seasonal river community with masonry and flood records. Marks water levels and hosts rites or markets. [The Met](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/essays/art-architecture-and-the-city-in-the-reign-of-amenhotep-iv-akhenaten-ca-13531336-b-c) |
| Nabataean Petra | Cistern Court and Cliff Gate | Desert trade route with actual catchment, workable rock and stored water. Increases water reliability and caravan access. [UNESCO Petra](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/326) |
| Medieval Champagne | Fair Hall and Market Square | Temperate crossroads with recurring trade and scheduled fairs. Draws traders and artisans. [UNESCO Provins](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/873) |
| Sichuan river engineering | Split-Flow Weir and Canal Park | Measured flow, slope, diversion and outfall. Improves irrigation subject to available water and maintenance. [UNESCO Dujiangyan](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1001/) |
| Rapa Nui cultural landscape | Headland Gathering Terrace | Rocky island and inter-island sea links. An original communal terrace after research on the particular period and community. [UNESCO Rapa Nui](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/715/) |
| Mississippian Cahokia | Raised Assembly Plaza | Floodplain with surplus and earthmoving labor. Raises a public platform above seasonal ground water. [UNESCO Cahokia](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/198/) |

Treat these as geographically and historically specific inspirations. Select deeper primary sources and local voices before adapting ceremonial imagery. Let learned styles move through migration, apprentices and trade. A people can develop a mixed building language. No ancestry-locked profession or universal trait.

Prototype the landmark score with existing buildings before creating full projects. First new vertical slice is the Flood Stair or Cistern Court because its purpose is physically verifiable. Qualification requires a real construction run, interrupted or saved mid-phase, visible massing from close/mid/wide camera, maintained local function, and a documented regional appearance.

## 4. Rival activity, surrender and city-state warfare

Keep the existing contextual player flow and physical rival missions. Add a reason trail for rival plans by society and directed pair. Report known access, motive, readiness, risk and the actual blocker. First reproduce absent raids on a mixed coast map. Verify a known target, a viable approach, a real crew, supplies, departure, encounter and either return or losses. Check AI-to-AI actions and reloads at sea. Use roles and tiers of seafaring earned by demonstrated rowing, navigation and rigging. An isolated early village can be incapable of a sea raid for explainable reasons.

A significant capitulation can open one clear dialog, only when defenders actually surrender or agree terms. Preview who controls the site, the actual stores, custody, garrison cost, stability, diplomatic fallout and expected civilian effects. Leverage comes from sustained occupation or disrupted supply, security, reserves and leadership rather than a scripted population threshold. Keep settlement control and terms independent where coherent.

- **Annex** transfers an intact settlement and queues governance with a real garrison and reconciliation cost.
- **Puppet** keeps local administration with enforceable obligations, autonomy and a real cost to impose terms.
- **Tribute** specifies annual cargo and a real route. Missed deliveries reopen negotiation or conflict. Payments require surplus and physical receipt.
- **Loot** loads actual goods into carriers with limited capacity. It can accompany certain terms, reduces reserves and worsens trust.
- **Captivity and forced labor** are represented through custody, resistance, escape, exchange, release and long-term diplomatic consequences. Model destruction and civilian loss through the settlement's actual state and resulting displacement. A devastated place loses labor, trade, future taxes and civic standing.

Treat conquest, defense and occupation as different tasks for commanders. Budget simulation work per active theater, stagger route searches, and preserve individual inventories and identities at all camera distances. Test defense, surrender, negotiated terms, refusal, intact transfer, withdrawal and supply recovery under rival-vs-rival and player involvement.

## 6. Execution order and acceptance

1. **Farm visibility and civic score.** Show the current cost/output and prove real landmarks contribute condition-weighted prestige, with repeat-build diminishing returns. Verify existing save reconstruction.
2. **Three physical production sites and a local meal fallback.** Measure full harvest throughput and test safe transfers and spoilage. Finish the existing water and riverbank work in parallel.
3. **Two towns with differentiated local stocks.** Complete and save a real founding journey and reciprocal shipments. Make labor pressure and site prospects visible, then add voluntary household relocation.
4. **Civic work and art slice.** Build the first phased landmark and animate one complete work chain. Test the original palette and assets on the M1 under the stated load before multiplying them.
5. **Rival seafaring and settlement outcomes.** Qualify actual hostile journeys and durable surrender terms, then exercise the 150/300 resident city and multiple town network.
6. **Broader culture, architecture and backlog.** Expand each researched regional family and role only after the shared simulation is sound. Keep the remaining task families in the governing audit, including save-safe work queues, construction clearance, dry and water cargo, naval combat, territory, social history, crop diversity and geographically varied maps.

These phases produce implementation evidence. The frozen 98-gate release checklist keeps its existing denominator and credit rules.

## 7. Generate wonders that have a reason to exist

Generate a **site-specific architectural brief** from watershed, sightlines, terrain envelope, useful approaches, building practice, materials, rituals, known neighbors and the settlement's recorded history. A planning model can propose several forms, names, material palettes and story hooks. A seeded rules engine chooses and validates a layout from a grammar of reusable parts. Record its seed, specification, asset references and generation version in the save. A second game must be able to generate a visibly different project from the same broad family. A reload must reproduce the exact one already built.

The grammar varies arrangement, entrances, profile, roof, retaining walls, negative space, work courtyards, terraces and construction sequence. It should yield layouts such as a long open river stair, a cliff-cut gate with split approaches, a courtyard cluster, an asymmetric dry cistern, and a wide earth-and-timber assembly terrace. Palette changes alone are insufficient. Generate several proposals offline, compare footprint silhouette, approach graph and roof profile against recent designs, and reject near duplicates. Start with six families × three footprint grammars × at least three material palettes and measure actual structural variety across 100 seeds. Use original AI concept art for review, then convert accepted parts to a small shared mesh library. Expensive asset generation belongs outside the simulation loop. Store the final approved family and module catalogue locally so campaign play and saves do not require a network connection.

Physical validators check foundation contacts, entrances, usable standing space, material loads, flood exposure, landmark silhouette and accessible work sites. Habitat profile controls which landmark makes sense, even within the same culture. One river polity can develop a modest fishing shrine in a tributary village, a monumental flood court in its capital, and a trading gate where a caravan crosses the watershed. Let the user approve silhouette families, material language, flags, armor and palette independently before the asset library changes.

## 8. Pottery and exchange

**Existing implementation.** Finite clay is surveyed at reachable freshwater, hauled, formed, dried and fired. Workshops have actual batches and weather affects drying. Maker marks, firing losses and water-vessel wear exist. Equipped jars modify carried water capacity. Individual filled trade containers and weighed payment are still design work.

Start with a heat-safe cooking pot, handled water jar, lidded dry-store jar and sealed transport vessel. Keep tare and contents separate. A filled vessel owns its contents until it physically unloads. Save fill amount, content kind, spoilage age, seal, condition, maker mark and custodian. Batch equivalent empty vessels for performance and create an individual identity when a vessel is filled, equipped or loaned. Transport uses actual cart, hull and worker capacity. Breakage creates documented loss or recoverable goods. Partial fills, cargo seizure, returnable containers and transfer after annexation must each conserve stock. Maker marks and shapes make regional pottery recognizable at trade and in storage. Capacity should follow vessel purpose and be tuned in-game. [The Met transport amphora](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/239940), [The Met hydriai](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/essays/greek-hydriai-water-jars-and-their-artistic-decoration), [early pottery residue study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4839459/)

Develop **barter, witnessed credit, weighed metal and minted currency** as overlapping options. A market can keep an account in a shared silver weight and settle a bill with goods. A merchant carrying a verified account can report prices and offer a future shipment. Use integer fine-metal mass, assay, local units and physical custody. Weighed silver serves recurring trade where both parties accept the standard. Gold is rare and useful for large contracts, civic work and treasury reserves. A developed mint needs real metal, labor, fuel, dies, stock and an issuer whose money people actually accept. Use issuer and quality in local quotes. Minting, melting, theft, fees, interest, defaults and change all need balanced entries. Record remaining balance and maturity separately from spendable cash. Gold and silver should never appear by opening a trade panel. [Bronze Age weight networks](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8271817/), [Kültepe silver loan](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/325858), [silver-account settlement with livestock](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/321691), [British Museum money gallery](https://www.britishmuseum.org/sites/default/files/2021-05/Money_Gallery_LPG_2020_Room_68.pdf)

Qualify a clay-to-port journey with four roles and vessel handovers first. Then validate silver-by-weight and an actual contract, including partial payment, a lost shipment, strict reload, failed assay and debt settlement. Add minted denominations after the measured exchange loop works. Keep bulk food, timber and other staples easy to trade throughout.

## 9. Culture, biomes and conversations

Give each biome a parameter sheet for seasonal precipitation, moisture, canopy, ground cover, soil depth, workable slopes, freshwater connectivity, winds, exposed rock, wildlife, crops and material sources. Climate and terrain determine what grows and can be harvested. Culture determines learned techniques, building conventions, clothing, heraldry and customs. A culture can span biomes, and trading neighbors can share methods while retaining different materials and appearance. Design connected transition areas and a dry, wet and cold example for every climate family. Retain the catalogue and playable access targets. Benchmark ecology updates by region and stagger scans across seasons.

Build conversation from **known facts, actual speakers and valid actions**. Maintain a compact memory of delivered goods, unresolved offers, weather losses, visitors, allies, family, work practice, travel and witnessed disputes. Use deterministic templates first. An optional model can phrase those facts, vary personality and propose dialogue choices mapped to existing validated commands. Dialogue cannot fabricate enemy stock, reveal fog, create a treaty, transfer goods or assign a job by itself. Cache authored styles and keep local fallbacks. Start with a tired worker describing a real bottleneck, a passing merchant giving route news and a concrete offer, a rival recounting a shared event, and a village elder explaining an actual monument. Keep occasional stories and intent, avoid repetitive banter. [Kültepe merchant letter](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/325849), [caravan accounts](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/325851)

## 11. Armor, flags, standing forces and first person

Armor starts as crafted layered textile or hide with a distinct visual silhouette. Add shields, helmets, scale or other period-appropriate protection as materials, production practice and repair facilities develop. The equipment record stores protection, movement burden, heat, wear, fit and material availability. Customization applies interchangeable material, dye and motif choices on a validated construction, with actual costs and a preview at gameplay distance. Let banners define a field, border and motif from a small editable vector grammar. Preview contrast and faction readability on cloth, shields, ships and the minimap. Save a compact recipe for each design.

Standing forces require trained people, upkeep, equipment and command roles. Retain temporary militia for local defense and auto stand-down. Assign garrisons and patrols through existing job and ownership systems. Show defensive duties and time away from production. Progression adds standing readiness, supply continuity and formation cohesion. Test whether better preparation changes deterrence and whether troops return or stay on the duty the player assigned.

Start first person as an optional walkabout of the same village with local inspection, simple task orders, pause and immediate overhead return. Then prototype a commander viewpoint that can select visible formations and issue existing orders through a command wheel. Use the same simulation, inventories, visibility and results from both views. Qualify motion comfort, navigation collision, UI accessibility and frame time first. Add richer close combat presentation after formations, armor and the camera are robust. [Bannerlord formation selection](https://www.taleworlds.com/en/News/536)

## 12. Approval and release boundaries

The concept sheets are review material. First choose or combine an interface direction, a world style, and a small group of landmark/flag/armor families. Produce the same nine in-game UI states as functional prototypes. Ship the approved visual direction only after a side-by-side play test and the Mac budget. Gameplay, historical research and terrain qualification can progress alongside the review. Keep the play build and player saves isolated from concept and asset trials.
