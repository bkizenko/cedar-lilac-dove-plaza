# Building and workplace catalogue

Current maintenance delta: standing buildings now support finite material-paid, physically hauled builder repairs; new worlds have light measured material/weather/use wear. This does not implement a dedicated repair-yard building, bridge maintenance, household beds or mortar manufacture. See [Building maintenance](BUILDING_MAINTENANCE_2026-11-03.md).

Current implementation delta: [Plant textiles and seasonal clothing](PLANT_TEXTILE_CHAIN_2026-11-02.md) adds paid bast spinning, weaving and light garments to the existing fibre shelter/tailor. The table below remains the dated October31 baseline; current executable recipes, station status and blockers come from `src/game/productionCatalog.ts` and `src/game/production.ts`. Advanced looms, flax crops, wool, dyes and repairs remain explicitly partial/planned.

Audit date: 2026-10-31. This is a source audit and implementation plan, not a claim that the missing chains have shipped. The current registry has **26 building IDs**, six age labels, and substantial early survival foundations. Clothing, hides, tanning, dedicated cooking, and distinct metal-smelting inputs are still absent. Keep the existing buildings and later-era roadmap while filling those real production gaps.

## Reference: what Dawn of Man actually documents

The developer's [official game description](https://dawnofman.madrugaworks.com/) explicitly connects hunting to meat, skins, bones, clothing and tools; gathering to water/wood/stone/ores; seasonal preparation to durable food and warm clothing; and domestication to food, materials and carrying power. Its campaign spans Stone to Iron, whereas Hearthwild deliberately continues through Classical and preserves Medieval/Renaissance. These are design references, not copied art or a claim of identical mechanics.

The [official FAQ](https://dawnofman.madrugaworks.com/faq.php) confirms that tanning requires **raw skins plus tannin**, that **skins and wool clothing provide warmth**, and that clothing counts include worn garments. It distinguishes goats/cattle for milk, sheep for wool, pigs for meat, and equids for hauling; it also describes herd slaughter limits, alert-manned towers, and tool/resource/workload prerequisites. This supports separate material inventories, equipped clothing, actual animal species and finite worker capacity. It does not justify a free wool supply or warming a village just by placing a named building.

The developer's [official Steam discussion for version 1.7](https://steamcommunity.com/app/858810/discussions/0/3011179244477989386/) documents cheese as a milk-preservation addition and states that bread is made at campfires and later ovens. Hearthwild's proposed dryers, butcher yard, tailor and later ovens below are its own executable design proposals. Exact Dawn of Man building recipes are not asserted where the official pages inspected do not publish them. The Steam “manual” link currently leads back to the developer FAQ, not a comprehensive building manual.

## How to read the audit

**Working** means a concrete code path exists for the stated effect. **Partial** means that the building exists and has some working effects, but the named broader chain is incomplete or abstract. Neither label independently passes the full campaign acceptance checklist; visual and sustained gameplay qualification remain separate. Construction material costs below are current base costs, before plot scaling, plans or bridge length. `W/S/C/I/F` mean logs/stone/copper/iron/food. A dash means no dedicated input/output or job was found, not an infinite supply.

All paid structures need physical construction labor; unpaid drawings provide no completed-building benefits. Two construction slots are ordinarily proposed per site. Ordinary labor remains subject to the explicit Auto work setting. Drinking-water dispatch is independent of that setting. Storage figures are additions to the existing stock/food-protection calculation, not individual physical bins with independent inventories.

## Town-hall placement: read-only reproduction and UX recommendation

The adjacent-hall ghost is consistent with an existing **75 m settlement-spacing rule**, not a flatness rejection. `placementIssue` calls `settlementIssue` before checking foundations/resources/overlap. With any own hall already present, a new hall within 75 m of any live own hall **or frontier outpost** is rejected with the current exact text: “A new settlement needs room beyond the current village”. Planned/unfinished halls count. The same function caps halls at five on all map sizes.

An isolated real-Game audit (`.play/townhall-placement-audit.log`) with dry flat ground reproduces: candidate at 14 m → spacing rejection; 74.9 m → same rejection; 75 m and 76 m near a valid resource clump → accepted placement checks. An unfinished first hall still blocks the adjacent second. On a separate dry 40% slope, town-hall placement returns no issue: the renderer raises the wall base to 72.43 m above a centre ground height of 70.4 m, with a footing down to 68.37 m. This confirms that ordinary hall placement currently has **no general slope/flatness rejection**. `dryFoundation` checks sampled flood-safe clearance; `foundationProfile` uses the highest rotated footprint sample, while the ghost is placed at centre ground height. This is source/fixture evidence; no claim is made to have inspected a new screenshot whose path was not supplied to this audit.

Recommended visible behavior, for the UI/placement owner to implement:

1. Display the numeric reason beside the ghost: **“New town: at least 75 m from a hall or outpost. Nearest: 14 m; move 61 m farther.”** Mark the nearest excluding structure and draw its 75 m boundary while placing a hall.
2. Offer the appropriate local alternative: **“To grow this village, build a Hut. A Town Hall starts another settlement.”** Keep multiple towns available rather than turning a confusing rejection into a one-town rule.
3. Show planned halls/outposts in that exclusion preview, and show the separate resource-clump requirement. Passing the 75 m rule does not waive dry ground, resources, access or overlap checks.
4. Show the actual next cost (first free; then 40/60/80/100 logs) and **towns established/planned versus cap**. A provisional map-size policy is 5 halls on 1040 m, 8 on 1560 m, 12 on 2080 m; this is a proposal requiring population/performance/supply qualification, not current behavior or a hidden change in this audit.
5. Match the ghost to the completed adaptive foundation height. If large retaining foundations become a gameplay constraint, expose measured footing rise/earthwork and a clear terrace action. Do not label the current 75 m rejection “not flat enough”. No general terrain flattening is authorized by this finding.

## Measurement and completion contract for the next chain batches

Each workplace must expose recipe inputs reserved/consumed/lost, finite work slots, actual assignee and arrival, hands-on work seconds, completion/partial output, storage capacity and condition, fuel/water consumption, job interruption/resumption, hauling/delivery, and explicit blocked reasons. Garments need manufactured/stored/equipped/worn-out counts and temperature protection; animals need species/sex/age/health/feed/water/pasture/keeper state; food needs raw/cooked/dried/smoked/salted lots and spoilage, with conserved inputs. Physical travel, a failed/blocked arrival, or an interrupted job must not fabricate output. Saves must preserve paid work and finite cargo exactly once.

Acceptance includes a normal first winter using actual collection, food, clothing, shelter, fuel and defenses, plus old-save reconstruction and desktop visuals at active work sites. The recent v12 water/fuel scenarios pass finite needs on two seeds, but seed 42 still loses a founder to a raid under the initial two-guard strategy; that stricter defense gap remains visible. This catalogue adds no passed items to the stable Classical-release checklist by itself.
