# Physical grain, flour and bread

Four delivered grain units are removed from their real pantry lot and the legacy food total once. An available adult at the camp cache carries the committed batch to a completed workplace, grinds it, then carries four distinct flour items back. Flour is not also edible food. Baking loads four flour, one delivered log and two litres of collected water, requires actual travel and hands-on work, then brings four edible bread units home. No extra physical item yield or passive building income is created.

The existing hearth remains the manual fallback: grinding takes60 model work seconds; baking takes45. A grinding stone costs2logs/6stone and takes24 work seconds per grinding batch. A stone bread oven costs8logs/8stone, takes24 work seconds per baking batch and has two staffed slots instead of the hearth's one. Both are Stone-stage physical buildings with ordinary construction; neither requires a large population or an abstract researcher. Dedicated grinding/baking does not require another hearth first. Actual completion time also includes travel and earned crafting practice.

`FOOD_NOURISHMENT.bread=1.2` is an explicit game-balance knob, not a historical or scientific measurement. At meals, the actual bread share supplies20% more usable nourishment per physical food unit than ordinary raw grain. The pantry still records and subtracts actual physical units eaten. Non-bread food consumption retains its previous calculation. This gives the prepared meal a practical benefit while preserving the4grain→4flour→4bread material chain. Bread also uses the ordinary edible-lot spoilage model. Raw-unit reserve/winter forecasts remain conservative because they omit the bread nourishment benefit.

Flour occupies the existing finite item capacity and expires as a whole lot after3600 simulation seconds. Whole-lot expiry avoids turning a newly produced four-flour batch into an unusable fraction before the player can load it. Paid flour batches expire after3600seconds; paid grain batches expire after7200. Paused work retains its original age and inputs. If the carrier dies before delivering, outputs are lost rather than credited remotely. Grain committed/lost, flour consumed/produced/spoiled/lost, fuel, water, edible outputs and lost edible loads are measured in production metrics. Mixed-age flour cache lots currently share the first stored freshness timestamp; separate per-batch storage containers remain future work.

The crafting shelter also has three shared equipment benches instead of the hall's one and a1.25 work-rate multiplier. Existing shorter shelter recipes remain intact. The multiplier is passed through the actual weapon, farm-tool, hauling-equipment and copper-trial executors. Practice accumulates actual hands-on seconds rather than bonus progress, and no age/population readiness threshold changes.

Compatibility: existing recipe IDs, paid durations/progress, food totals and item keys remain valid. New flour/bread/metric fields are optional or accepted through the authoritative recipe/item/food registries. `pauseProductionProject(g,id)` and `resumeProduction(g,id)` operate on actual committed work rather than speculative queue rows. `productionWorkSeconds` and `productionSlots` are the UI's authoritative model values. Catalogue and selected-workplace controls expose real recipes; unrelated proposed industry remains explicitly planned.

Focused tests cover a full physical hearth chain, exact grain/fuel/water debit and delayed edible credit, a mid-work saved interruption with a replacement adult, actual paid construction and processing at both dedicated buildings without another hearth, whole flour expiry, missing-grain rejection, a lost bread carrier, nourishment conservation and shelter work/bench advantages. The existing eight hunting/hide/clothing/production tests also pass. Browser visuals, natural-campaign food balance and fully simulated per-container storage are separate qualification gates.
