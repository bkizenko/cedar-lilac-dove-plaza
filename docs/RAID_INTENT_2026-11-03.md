# Limited raid intentions and severity

Base8836777 plus the separately frozen river-objective correction. This package changes only newly spawned sea-party objectives. Old in-flight records without `seaRaid.plan` retain their previous objective/combat rules; no old party is silently converted into a peaceful scout.

## Behavior

New parties receive a version1 plan bound to the actual actorId and a shared bandId. A seeded band roll uses difficulty.band plus the existing dangerous-conflict setting; it draws no extra global random values. Tiny first-wave bands of at most two people scout. Subsequent intent partitions support loot, conditional capture, destruction and rare annihilation. At Normal severity, the nominal selection weights are67.65% loot,18% capture,14% destruction and0.35% annihilation. The hardest ordinary profile raises annihilation to2.45%; dangerous conflict adds a bounded severity increment. These are selection probabilities, not measured campaign frequencies, and actual capture can downgrade to loot when physical custody is unavailable.

Scouts observe actual local structures, residents and defenders, then withdraw. Limited looters approach an actually observed standing warehouse/hall, reach its real entrance and spend3seconds collecting. Only then do they inspect available supplies, debit an exact edible pantry lot or finite primitive resource, and carry a bounded4–8unit quota subject to carrying capacity. They can leave with supplies without destroying a building. Exact-cache collection and repeated-call conservation are tested separately from the full ordinary-step physical return.

Nonlethal plans protect workers and leaders unless they are actually attacking within combat reach. Armed interception remains possible. Destructive plans can damage structures while protecting noncombatants, then collect finite ruin goods and withdraw. Rare annihilation plans do not interrupt destruction merely to collect ruin goods. Wounds, local force imbalance and the existing departure deadline can still force even a severe band to retreat; this is an objective, not a guarantee to annihilate a village.

Capture is deliberately conservative: a real nearby reachable owned raider camp must exist, the target must be an actual unarmed adult without a conflicting paid commitment, and the ordinary local-superiority/surrender/guard rules must accept it. Existing carried food can provision the same captive. The same guard and person physically travel to camp and enter guarded custody; there is no abstract abduction/despawn or naval captive teleport. Real raider huts now expose their already-existing camp cache through the shared supply/drop model, matching their existing stolen-goods return role. New-plan escort completion is recognized before generic guard duty only after both bodies actually reach the custody camp.

## Return, backpressure and measurements

Existing owned-vessel boarding/sailing/disembarkation and actual overland return remain authoritative. A legacy shore-spawned party without a usable hull/refuge stays visibly stranded with its original body and cargo. For new plans only, this is recorded as a stranded withdrawal rather than a false home return. It ends active-invasion/respite accounting, so the first harmless scout does not block every subsequent raid forever. Twelve stranded people create arrival backpressure; an already-arriving band can take that count above the threshold, but no person is deleted. Actual later boarding/return can resume. Legacy parties without new plans retain their previous campaign-blocking behavior.

Plans retain bounded dated local observations and actual collection/withdrawal/capture metrics. `raidIntentReport` returns copies for read-only inspection. A bounded256event history distinguishes real return, loss and stranding, retaining actor identity and actual cargo. Validation checks plan fields, required metrics, actor linkage, timestamps, coordinate conditions for active stranding, bounded lists and duplicate event identity. Validation/reporting consumes no random draws.
