# Essential roles as a settlement grows

Design guidance, not automatic building unlocks, population quotas or a claim that large settlements are qualified. Population includes dependents: the actual available adults, rest, travel, season and concurrent work determine capacity. A physical need does not require a separate full-time occupation or one building per person. The same person can gather, cook, repair and teach at different times; an occupied bench or distant haul still takes real time.

| Scale | Essential work and sensible organization | Physical places and current boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Hamlet,5–20 people | Secure food/water/fuel, shared shelter, occasional hunting/preparation, clothes/tool repair, seasonal crops/animal care, lookout/defense. Most adults rotate through several tasks. Protect a subsistence reserve before adding long projects. | Shared cache/hearth, huts, accessible work surfaces/racks and tool shelter; field/pen only with real inputs and carers. Gathering, water, shelter, crafting and basic farming/goats exist. New cooking/hide/clothing batches need their connected qualification; full preservation/pasture remain incomplete. |
| Village,20–100 | Add reliable food preservation, planned spring/autumn labor, store care, construction/repair, carrying and occasional exchange. Several practiced craft workers can make equipment part-time and teach apprentices while working. Fifty people do not need dedicated researchers. | Share craft benches, ovens/racks/vats as actual demand justifies them; stores, routes and sufficient shelter matter more than a civic monument. Existing construction, benches, physical trade and practical experiments are foundations; full learning/apprenticeship, food processing, sanitation and maintenance chains remain incomplete. |
| Town,100–500 | Recurring surplus can support specialist makers, organized hauling, traders, builders and guards. Reserve workers for harvests and emergencies. Add maintained water/waste handling, care for sick residents and coordination of supplies. | Several supplied neighborhoods/workplaces, larger stores and real transport connections. Existing multiple towns/markets/temples are partial foundations; comprehensive water/waste, healthcare, apprenticeship and staffed civic services are needed. Do not infer these from building names. |
| City,500+ | Coordinate sustained food/fuel/material imports, distribution, infrastructure upkeep, skilled workshops, care, defense and public services. Specialists depend on measured surplus and transport throughput. | Dense supplied districts, redundant water/storage/routes and staffed institutions. This is a later performance and simulation qualification target, not a currently proven playable population. |

Innovation should arise from actual problems, observed materials, attempts, repeated practice and teaching. Part-time craft apprenticeship is the default early pattern. Dedicated teachers or experimenters become affordable only when actual demand, adult capacity and reliable surplus support them; there is no fixed research tax at50 people. Workload should expose these tradeoffs as person-time and actual waiting, rather than spawn a new mandatory profession for every proposed building.
