Profiles and practice/service parameters live in `boatClasses.ts`. Legacy hulls keep absent `hullClass`, five seats,300HP, original speed, original paid duration and no new wear. New fields stay optional; loading an old hull does not infer historical voyage credit or rewrite its class.

Only a successful steering commit on real water records distance. Captains count once per vessel; passengers do not multiply rowing experience. Actual carried goods retain a bounded saved water-leg stamp. A physical friendly-cache delivery grants at most the original stamped quantity once, after at least20wet metres. Holds, sightings, elapsed time and unfinished bench projects grant no voyage practice. Remote callbacks retain the stamp without credit. Transfer, lost ship, changed owner and dead/removed carrier reconciliation prevent duplicated credit. The cleanup hook runs before body removal. Seeing a captured advanced hull does not invent local production history.

A scout prototype requires80wet metres and8waterborne goods actually delivered. A freight prototype requires300loaded wet metres and40delivered goods; loaded means measured vessel burden≥0.2, or a personal canoe actually carrying cargo. Read-only Knowledge shows need, recorded observations/timestamps, paid prototype progress, actual completed hulls, demonstrated cargo return and current adoption. The paid physical hull is the experiment, not an abstract research queue. An undemonstrated surviving prototype blocks repeated adoption; a lost prototype can be retried with paid inputs. Actual prototype cargo return proves repeatable use. The first prototype never appears as a reward.

Construction and service reuse `HaulageProject` ownership, shared dock benches and actual adult effort. New saved `hullOrder` records class/mode/exact paid inputs/target boat. Materials are checked before reservation and debited once; cordage comes from actual item stock. Explicit interruption, save/load and a replacement worker preserve the same paid project and progress. Paused/active service reserves the exact hull; manual sailing/exploration/boarding and automatic coastal dispatch cannot take it. A paid voyage also prevents concurrent service. Recall of a reserved moored hull attempts physical disembarkation, rather than queuing impossible sailing behind its own service lock. Lost-hull service can be explicitly abandoned without refund. Native commissioning selects a new class only when both earned and actually affordable, otherwise retaining its legacy ferry; it creates no cordage. Autonomous NPC cordage production/adoption frequency is not qualified here.

New hulls wear from actual wet distance and measured load: scout0.008HP/metre, punt0.004, multiplied by1+burden. Holds do not wear a hull. Condition changes real speed. A named25% wear floor permits limping home and never heals an already-damaged hull below that floor. Paid service costs wood/cordage proportional to the recorded deficit (0.4of build cost, whole units, minimum1each) and60actual work seconds. It requires the same owned hull physically moored within18m of its actual service dock and a maker at that dock bench. A remote, captured or lost target cannot complete; no replacement hull is spawned. Completion restores only the health amount funded at admission, preserving crew/cargo. A small numeric tolerance makes a genuinely complete repair exactly full instead of leaving an infinitesimal repeat-service offer.

Original sculpted scout/outrigger and braced-punt geometries use two bounded instanced meshes, at most96instances/class and no extra lights. All art fits the existing3×6m navigation envelope. Eight real passenger offsets fit it as well. No fake cargo stacks or naval combat are introduced. Legacy rendering/picking remains on the existing mesh; new-class picking resolves current actual hull references and refreshes after same-ID imports. Workload/craft-site labels identify real class construction or service; the existing actual bench rate and time estimates remain shared.
