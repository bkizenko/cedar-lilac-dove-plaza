# Finite clay and useful fired vessels

Stone-age pottery is an actual physical chain, not an age reward. Survey with a resident near a known, reachable freshwater river/lake bank. Only low, dry, gently sloping bank locations sampled by the explicit alluvial-potential rule can expose clay. This is a tuning model, not a claim of geological prospecting accuracy. Surveying records an initially finite 80–160-unit deposit; repeat surveys cannot replenish it. Four clay take 24 work seconds to dig, then a person carries them to the actual cache. One reserved digger per bank; interruption retains the carried load and remaining deposit.

| Step | Manual/shared method | Dedicated method | Conservation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Form | Hearth:90 work seconds | Pottery shelter:30 seconds,2 slots | 4 clay +2 actual water →4 greenware |
| Dry | Existing rack:10 loading seconds, then 90 effective weather seconds | Pottery shelter: same loading/exposure, covered against rain | 4 greenware →4 dried unfired ware, physical pickup/delivery |
| Fire | Hearth:90 work seconds,4 logs | Kiln:45 work seconds,2 logs,2 slots | 4 dried ware →0–4 vessels; other units become real potsherds |

Rain rewets exposed greenware;180 cumulative wet seconds damages the paid batch. Freezing prevents useful drying. Humidity changes the rate, so 90 effective dry seconds can take several minutes. Shelter protects against rain, not low temperatures. Loaded drying keeps its slot but releases its worker. Interrupted and saved projects retain paid inputs, exposure and progress; a replacement can continue material already at a workplace after a maker dies.

Firing losses are a deterministic, saved batch-quality tuning model: occasional complete failures; routine hearth firing loses one vessel, while kiln firing has fewer losses. Inputs/fuel remain consumed, and shards occupy actual item storage. This is not a heat/atmosphere/refractory simulation. The recipe preview states a maximum, and storage reports use actual batch outputs.

At the cache, assign one delivered fired vessel to a resident. It leaves communal stock and becomes that person's owned `waterVessel`. Actual water collection then carries 32 L instead of 24 L, with 16 instead of 12 filling seconds: the benefit is fewer travel trips, not created water or increased filling speed. Ten successful delivered trips wear the vessel out. Breakage/death records a lost vessel, does not duplicate pottery, and removes the visual jar. Residents without pottery keep their old water behavior.

Paid firing records producing society/site and a deterministic local style, retained in delivered stock provenance and transferred to an equipped carrier. Existing unrecorded/editor-granted pottery is explicitly unrecorded rather than assigned fabricated history. Vessel trade, whole-pot transport mass, clay mineral chemistry, wheel throwing, glazes, ceramic food storage, kiln heat cycles and shard recycling remain incomplete.

Registry now has 36 structures and 23 processing recipes. The 25 functional roles and 106 station functions remain grouping metadata. Pottery shelter costs 8 logs/2 stone and 14 base construction seconds; kiln 8 logs/8 stone and 20 seconds. Both must be paid, reached and completed. No world-generation resource hash is changed, no terrain is flattened, and no clay or pottery is awarded merely for constructing either workplace.
