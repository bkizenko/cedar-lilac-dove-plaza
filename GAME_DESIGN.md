# Dawn of Empire — master game specification

## Concept and scope
A real-time, single-player 3D tribe strategy game. A small community survives seasons, develops specialized work, and decides when to withdraw workers into a militia or send them to conquer resource regions. The distinctive decision is that the people defending the village are also its productive economy; war consumes both stores and working time. Territory, exchange and seasonal reserves matter alongside army size.

Keep the existing browser/PC delivery and Three.js engine. Target keyboard and mouse equally; a complete session must be playable without a pointer. No paid assets, accounts, multiplayer, reproduction of commercial soundtracks or new engine. Existing later ages remain compatible, but the first balanced campaign ends at Iron; later eras are optional future content.

## Session and loops
A target skirmish lasts 35–60 minutes with pause and 1×/2×/4× speed. First 0–10 minutes: forage, choose safe tree/stone sites, place huts, store food and scout. Minutes 10–25: farms/storage, workshop tools, spears/bows, defend the first raid, trade or contest a neighboring deposit. Minutes 25–60: metallurgy, specialist armies, outposts, siege/raids and regional control.

Every minute: inspect stores/idle labor → assign work → transport resources → build or train → scout threats → react. Long term: survive winter → specialize → expand routes → choose economic/military research → defend or conquer. Tension comes from food consumption, cold, travel distance, raids and missing workers. Avoid surprise losses: forecast at least 60 seconds of food shortage and 30 seconds of raid warning. Defeat: no surviving people and no recoverable settlement, or lost main camp with no replacement after grace period. Victory: subdue opposing settled tribes OR control the target regions for five minutes while sustaining food and population. Keep existing victory rules until this condition is implemented and tested. Offer continue-after-victory.

## World
Preserve the seeded 520-unit island heightfield. Build explicit biome masks for grassland, conifer forest, deciduous forest, rocky upland and wet shore; fertile soil is separate from slope. Water is impassable except docks/boats in later content. Safe starts need food within 15m, wood within 25m, stone within 40m and two routes out. Put scarce ore beyond home reserves. Regions grant measured production benefits rather than conjuring all economy. Navigation and placement sample the same height/slope/water masks. Boundaries are visible coastline/cliffs. Terrain edit/destruction does not alter the heightfield in alpha.

## Camera and commands
Keep pan/zoom/yaw/pitch with clamps and focus. WASD/arrows pan in camera space, Q/E rotate, PageUp/PageDown zoom. Optional edge scroll off by default to prevent UI navigation drift. F home, I idle worker, C next owned unit (Shift reverses), B next owned building, V all military, J all workers. K switches keyboard ground cursor; cursor stays at screen center while camera moves. Enter selects/places; R contextual move/gather/attack/rally; Z attack-move at cursor; period stop. Shift+Enter toggles selection. Ctrl+1–9 stores groups, Alt+1–9 recalls without stealing building keys. Tab/Shift+Tab navigate native UI, Enter/Space activate focused UI; gameplay shortcuts must never intercept editable fields or focused buttons. Escape cancels placement/menu and returns focus to world. Brackets cycle visible resource targets. 1–8 retain quick buildings; every other building/action stays keyboard reachable through HUD. Left click selects, shift adds, screen-space drag selects; right click contexts, drag RMB rotates, middle drag pans. Later: Shift queue, patrol, line/wedge/loose formations. Save/load and credits must be reachable from keyboard.

## Resources and work
Keep five stockpiles: food, wood, stone, copper, iron. Flint is represented by stone-grade tools; hide/bone/clothing initially become equipment/upkeep modifiers, not extra stockpiles. Tools/weapons are workshop inventories (existing spears/bows/blades), not terrain resources. Unit carry limits and hauling to finished friendly storage remain mandatory. Gathering depletes nodes; farms/regrowth replenish slowly. Buildings do not create materials without assigned labor except explicitly labeled regional income.

Needs: food per villager; warmth/shelter and tool quality modify work, rather than independently micromanaged thirst/sleep meters. Task priority: explicit orders > escape immediate hazard > finish hauling > construction/repair > assigned job > automatic nearest affordable job. Manual job locks persist until canceled. Failed tasks report reasons and choose a reachable alternative; never teleport. Population growth needs housing and a food surplus; recruitment consumes food and build queue time. Death releases jobs/equipment and housing. Hunt deer/goat; boar can fight back. Predators (wolf) are later hazards; birds cosmetic. Herds flee nearby attack, respawn at distant valid habitat, migrate between seasonal feeding regions only after profiling.

## Construction and production
Place ghost snapped to 2m grid. Validate bounds, terrain, slope, overlap, resources, age and site requirements before spending once. Reserve footprint immediately; builders must approach a reachable perimeter and increase completion only while adjacent. Show progress, assigned workers and blocked reason. Cancel refunds unbuilt fraction; repair consumes proportional materials, requires builder, stops under attack. Destruction clears navigation occupancy and production queues. Rally points accept ground or resource target.

Existing costs in constants.ts are authoritative until balance passes. Proposed additions: granary 60 wood/15 stone (food capacity +100; Stone), workshop 80 wood/30 stone (tools, spear/bow crafting; Stone), wall 8 wood/4 stone per 2m segment (Stone), gate 30 wood/15 stone (Stone), archery range 90 wood/25 stone (Bronze). Reuse warehouse as generic storage and quarry as stone workplace rather than add redundant stone-storage UI. Huts house people; farms need fertile land; lumber camps improve nearby chopping; granaries improve food hauling; barracks train melee; range trains archers; forge enables metals; towers cover approaches. All prerequisites, footprint and costs belong in one definition table, with HUD reading it.

## Research
Target three campaign ages: Stone (survival, hunting, wood buildings), Bronze (cultivation, copper tools, shielded spear, organized units), Iron (armor, advanced warriors, stronger defenses). Within each: economic vs martial picks with visible costs and effects. Data fields: id, age, prerequisites[], cost, duration, effects[], exclusiveGroup?. Apply modifiers through derived stats, never cumulative edits on reload. Existing later age values are retained for saved-game compatibility until explicit migration. Research, queued state and branch choices serialize.

## Military and combat
Retain existing melee/spear/archer definitions. Workers/hunters can defend but lose gather time. Spear: cheap reach, bonus vs mounted. Archer: range and vulnerable in melee. Shield warrior: resistance to arrows, moderate speed. Advanced warrior: expensive iron and upkeep, no universal dominance. Cavalry is optional post-prehistoric content. Use soft counters (+25–50%), armor subtraction with minimum damage 1, never immunity. Weapon reach plus target radius determines engagement. Ranged attacks launch physical visual projectiles; deterministic hit is acceptable initially; elevation modifies range slightly, not random misses. Cooldowns use simulation time. Reacquire targets at a budgeted frequency. Hold should attack in range without chasing; stop cancels orders; attack-move resumes its destination after target death. Retreat is an explicit movement order with no automatic retaliation. Buildings can be targeted and towers use the same damage rules.

Group movement assigns stable slots, paths to shared corridor, then local separation. Queue routes preserve waypoints; narrow passages compress formation without blocking forever. Dead units become non-colliding immediately but fade through a 0.8–1.4s death pose. Pool arrows, impacts, dust and corpses. Fire indicates building damage; blood optional and off by default.

## AI
Strategic director (1–2Hz staggered per tribe): assess food/labor/threat, choose build/train/research/scout/defend/raid, assign budgets and retreat below strength threshold. Individual workers/combat units run in simulation with target scans 5Hz. AI must spend actual stock, use build queues, respect fog, recover workers/storage before attack, and not know hidden armies. Presets alter decision delays and aggression, not resource cheats. Separate economy planner, military planner and local steering incrementally behind Game methods.

## Navigation and visibility
A* on occupancy grid with no diagonal corner cutting; unit-radius-aware checks; bounded path jobs, cached route per order and occupancy revision. Coarse long-distance corridor then fine local avoidance if needed. Never teleport across water/obstacles. Failed route returns reachable fallback plus UI message. Buildings dirty a region; trees use collision only where trunks prevent movement. Spatial hash supports separation, resource/target searches and vision stamps.

Fog has unseen/explored/current states. Friendly units/buildings reveal current cells; history persists. Enemy entities must not be selectable/targetable/rendered live outside current visibility. Remember last seen buildings as noninteractive ghosts later. Minimap uses the same visibility contract. Keyboard target cycling respects it.

## UI, saves and audio
HUD: stock/capacity, food trend, population, selected unit HP/task/carry, production queue, build costs/prerequisites, research, warnings, time/speed, diplomacy and minimap. Use bronze/parchment tokens, readable type, visible focus, accessible button labels, stable tab order and a concise persistent keyboard guide. Avoid keyboard traps; pause while reading complex help.

Save: versioned envelope, seed/world data, all mutable state, IDs for references, queues, resources, weather, wildlife, regions, routes, technology, timers, vision and UI-independent player state. Decode/validate before changing live game. Two slots (primary/backup), never overwrite good backup with corrupt data. Autosave only after entering/resuming; manual save/load with feedback. Load paused. Preserve unknown-version saves and explain incompatibility; no silent resets. Replay determinism is a separate milestone requiring a serialized PRNG.

Music: recorded or sampled real instruments, no synthesized pad score. Village starts sparse flute/lute/fiddle; settlement growth introduces fuller folk adventure; advanced ages/external campaigns introduce orchestral strings/brass/percussion. Nearby combat overrides to battle, then 20s calm before returning. Crossfade 4s, minimum dwell 20s; pause/hidden mute; separate music/SFX volumes; no autoplay before gesture; local media supports offline play after caching. Credit composer/source/license in game and THIRD_PARTY_ASSETS.md. Sound effects: natural work/animal/environment layers with voice limits, distance attenuation and sparse UI cues. No commercial game soundtrack reuse.

## Vertical slice acceptance
One readable real 3D map with vegetation/stone/water; start from title entirely by keyboard; select individuals/groups; pan/rotate/zoom; gather and haul wood/stone/food; place and finish hut; recruit; train spear and archer; defend and attack enemy; death feedback; fog/minimap; recorded music progression; save/reload construction and active orders. Complete a 20-minute session without exceptions or mouse. Profile at 80 villagers + 60 friendly military + 100 enemies + 40 animals + 100 buildings + 2,000 scenery instances. No claim of completion until all gates pass.

## Performance and release
PC target 1080p, midrange integrated/discrete GPU, 60fps typical, 30fps worst battle, p95 simulation <8ms and render CPU <6ms. Alpha cap 240 active people total, 40 animals, 100 buildings, 2,000 instanced resources; aim <250 draw calls high/<120 low. Skinned animation 15–30Hz at distance; AI staggered; spatial hash replaces quadratic collisions. Main risks: scene draw count, main-thread path searches, global scans, snapshot/HUD allocation and large media decode. Build/typecheck/unit regressions, desktop/mobile smoke, keyboard workflow, save corruption cases, fog leak tests and long-session profiling are release gates. Full alpha requires a complete match plus reload, stress and balance checks, not just a screenshot.
